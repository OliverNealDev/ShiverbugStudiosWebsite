Accessibility Statement

We want this site to work for everyone who lands on it, including people using a keyboard, a screen reader, magnification, or a browser set to cut out motion. Here's where we actually are. Last updated 2 August 2026.

How Accessible This Site Is

We aim to meet WCAG 2.2 Level AA, the international standard for web accessibility.

Every page on this site meets Level AA as far as our own testing shows. We've assessed this ourselves rather than commissioning an external audit, so if you find something we've missed, we'd genuinely like to know.

What We've Done

Every page works with a keyboard alone, with a visible outline showing where you are and a skip link so you can jump past the menu. Pages below the home page open with a back button, so there's always one obvious way out that doesn't depend on your browser's. The gallery viewer keeps focus inside itself while open, closes on Esc, and puts you back where you started.

Text meets the 4.5:1 contrast ratio the standard asks for. Pages reflow to a 320px screen without sideways scrolling, cope with doubled text size and increased letter spacing, and every image either has alternative text or is marked as decorative. Buttons and links are at least 44 by 44 pixels, apart from links inside a sentence and the three contact links in the press kit fact sheet, which are smaller but sit far enough from anything else to be hard to miss.

If your device is set to reduce motion, the scroll animations and the looping gallery clips stop on their own and never start. If it isn't, every looping clip carries its own pause button in the corner of the clip, which stops and restarts that clip.

What Isn't Right Yet

A few team profiles are unfinished. Where we haven't written someone up yet, their tile on the team pages isn't a link. Selecting it shows a short note saying so instead of taking you to an empty page. Those tiles work with a keyboard and announce themselves as buttons, but if you have JavaScript turned off they are simply not selectable, and the note stays hidden. The original profile pages still exist so older links keep working.

The looping clips in the co-development galleries have no captions. They're silent and carry no spoken or written information, so there's nothing to caption, but they aren't described in as much detail as the still images are. The Out of Water trailer on the press page is a download rather than something that plays on the site; when the new trailer is embedded here it will have captions and a written description of what it shows.

We've only tested with one screen reader, NVDA on Windows. We haven't tried JAWS or VoiceOver, and they don't always behave the same way. We also haven't tested Windows High Contrast Mode.

How We Tested This

We checked all 25 pages rather than a sample. Contrast, heading order, alternative text, form labelling, link naming, target sizes and focus behaviour were measured on every page; keyboard operation and the gallery viewer were tested by hand; and the site was read through with NVDA. This is our own assessment, not an independent audit.

Tell Us If Something Doesn't Work

If any part of this site gets in your way, email contact@shiverbugstudios.com and tell us what happened and what you were using. We'll reply and fix what we can, and if you need something here in a different format, just ask. We're a small studio, so that's a real person reading it.

The Legal Bit

Shiverbug Studios Ltd is a private company, so the Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) Accessibility Regulations 2018 don't apply to us. We still have duties under the Equality Act 2010 to make reasonable adjustments, and we'd want to do this regardless. This statement is voluntary, and written to be honest rather than flattering.
